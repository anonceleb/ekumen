CBLR and AEO explained

Two separate things get bundled together in conversation: who is legally allowed to clear your goods through customs, and whether your compliance history earns you faster treatment once they do. They're governed by different rules entirely.

CBLR: who may act as a customs broker

Under the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations, 2018 (CBLR), only a licensed Customs Broker may transact customs clearance business on behalf of an importer or exporter. It's the single hardest licence to obtain in Indian freight forwarding, which is exactly what makes it valuable to hold.

Getting one requires meeting eligibility conditions (Indian citizenship, a clean record, a relevant degree or professional qualification, and certified financial viability), passing a written and oral examination covering the Customs Act, valuation, classification and trade documentation, and being granted a licence valid — with intimation — across all customs stations in India.

The card system underneath it

  • F Card — held by the licence holder personally, with full authority to sign customs documents.
  • G Card — an employee authorised to transact customs business after passing a separate departmental exam. G-card holders do most of the actual filing work in any brokerage, and they're consistently scarce.
  • H Card — an employee permitted limited work, such as document collection or delivery, with no signing authority.

The obligations attached to the licence are real and enforced: verifying a client's identity and address independently, advising clients to comply with the law, not withholding information from Customs, and maintaining records. Letting another party file under your licence for a fee — sub-letting — is the most common cause of licence revocation in India.

AEO: a status, not a licence

India's Authorised Economic Operator programme is voluntary. It grants graduated trade-facilitation benefits — reduced examination rates, faster clearance, and partial-to-full waivers on bank guarantees — to supply chain operators with a demonstrated record of compliance.

TierWho it's forWhat it rewards
AEO-T1Importers and exportersA documentary application with no physical audit — the lightest tier to obtain
AEO-T2Importers and exporters with stronger systemsA mandatory on-site audit; unlocks deferred duty payment and self-sealing
AEO-T3Track-record T2 holdersThe deepest scrutiny and the highest tier of facilitation
AEO-LOLogistics operators — forwarders, customs brokers, custodians, terminalsWaiver of bank guarantees for transhipment and reduced scrutiny on the operator's own activity

The distinction that matters: a CB licence is what lets you legally do the work. AEO status is what your compliance track record earns you once you've been doing it cleanly for a while — and for a logistics operator with roughly a year of clean filing history behind it, applying for AEO-LO is a largely one-time documentation effort with a permanent, ongoing payoff in clearance speed and reduced bank guarantee exposure.
