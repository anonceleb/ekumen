Reducing a claim to a yes or no is harder than it looks

didyoubeattheindex.com took a couple of hours to build. The question it answers takes about as long to read: did your portfolio actually do better than just buying the index, over the period you held it. Type in the numbers, get a yes or no.

A couple of hours of build time is a strange thing to be proud of, and also worth being honest about. It reads as a flex, and it partly is one. But the part that stuck with us wasn't the build. It was what has to be true before a yes-or-no answer is allowed to exist at all.

Most claims don't want to be yes or no

Most interesting questions resist a binary answer, and the honest response to most of them is "it depends." That's usually correct, and it's also usually where a product idea quietly dies, because "it depends" isn't something you can put a claim behind.

The appeal of "did you beat the index" is that it refuses the hedge. Someone lands on the page wanting one of two words back. Building toward that means giving up every comfortable qualifier before you write a line of code, not after.

Where the real hours actually went

The interface took no time at all. The time went into deciding what the comparison was even allowed to mean: which benchmark, over which window, measured how. Get any of that quietly wrong and the yes or no is still confident, still fast, and now also false.

That's the part that doesn't show up in a screenshot. A two-hour project can hide a much longer argument you had with yourself about whether the claim underneath it actually holds.

The reward wasn't the launch

The satisfying part wasn't shipping. It was the moment before that, when the question finally sat still long enough to be answerable cleanly, and we could see all the way through it. Everything after that was just typing.

We're not holding this up as a template. Most problems worth solving won't compress into one honest yes or no, and forcing them to would be its own kind of dishonesty. But when a problem does compress that cleanly, chasing that clarity down is a genuinely good use of a couple of hours.
